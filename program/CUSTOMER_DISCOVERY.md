# HAVEN customer discovery — no-payments, no-preorders research protocol

Goal: determine whether families will actually choose HAVEN over real alternatives at the proposed prices, and which features they will trade away to keep that price.

## Recruiting

Target 30 interviews across at least these segments: recent infant / toddler parents using rear-facing restraints; multiple-children households; families with adolescents and extracurricular sports; budget-constrained buyers; parents who regularly transport pets; shoppers considering midsize SUV upgrades. Avoid only sampling friends/fans. Offer privacy-conscious incentives within budget. Obtain consent before recording and do not collect children's names or identifying health data.

Collect comparison model, budget, size needs, child-seat types (not children's personal data), ownership frustrations and real buying timeline.

## Interview order (20 minutes)

1. Tell me about the last time loading children or sports gear into your vehicle was frustrating.
2. What vehicle do you use now and what made you buy it? What would make you replace it?
3. What's the most frightening situation you've faced in a car? How did you handle it?
4. When you choose a family SUV, which *three* facts make a dealership shortlist?
5. Which safety technologies do you know work, and which claims do you distrust?
6. What are your actual child-seat fit and installation challenges? Rear-facing, multiple seats, side access?
7. What do you wish the second row could do that yours can't? Would you rather have an entertainment screen or bring your own tablet?
8. Are a removable vacuum, passive cooler, active cooler, tinted windows or massaging seats compelling? Ask for recent examples, not yes/no validation.
9. Which would you remove from a $39,995 vehicle to reduce price by $1,500?
10. Which standard feature would change your decision about buying another brand?
11. If HAVEN were unknown, what third-party safety proof, warranty and service options would you require?
12. What price, dealership/service footprint and warranty would make you trust a newly formed brand? What would prevent purchase regardless of features?

Finish with a blind comparison of a real competitor and HAVEN spec, clearly indicating HAVEN is not production ready. Ask respondent to choose and explain; don't push for agreement.

## Survey follow-up

- Identify actual next-car budget and timing.
- Force-rank 8 feature groups and use price tradeoffs.
- Separate speculative desire to own HAVEN from a *financially and logistically qualified* willingness to pay.
- No reservation deposits or promises of delivery.
- Track sample biases; report negative feedback and dissenting opinions.

## Evidence artifacts

An anonymized interview table and synthesis: quote (permission-based and de-identified), recurring problem, severity, willingness to pay, alternative solutions, design consequence, unresolved objections. Create a purchase-criteria map by household type. Publish only aggregate data after appropriate consent.

## Stop / pivot criteria

- Buyers value advanced safety but are not willing to trade any premium feature for cost.
- Buyers don't trust an unproven brand without proven service and crash results.
- Seat packaging requirements conflict with available licensed architectures.
- Unit economics cannot meet target when actual engineering and warranty reserves are included.
- Supplier terms make critical safety technology unavailable or nonviable at planned volumes.

No customer discovery alone proves product-market fit for a new vehicle manufacturer.
