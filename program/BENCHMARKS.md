# HAVEN Core & Reserve — external benchmark baseline (2026-10-11)

## Why a benchmark must be measured, not invented

"World's safest SUV" is not an objective certification available from a single test provider. Benchmark across (1) IIHS crash / rear-seat ratings, (2) U.S. NHTSA NCAP, (3) Euro NCAP consumer testing and child-presence protocols, (4) independent injury-risk and pedestrian compatibility metrics, and (5) real-world safety system behavior. Only an engineer and certified test laboratory can create a valid HAVEN comparison.

### Required Core evidence against benchmarks

1. **Crash:** pass applicable FMVSS; seek highest achievable independent 2026-style IIHS front/side and rear-occupant outcomes. No "five-star" statement until tested.
2. **Child retention:** outboard and center CRS configurations studied using representative U.S. rear-facing and forward-facing seats. Check anchor visibility, belt geometry and buckles.
3. **Heat:** direct infant/child presence detection under blankets, through child seats and with cabin power supply partially degraded; correct alert escalation without false guarantees of cooling.
4. **Driver assistance:** validated standard AEB, pedestrian detection, lane discipline and blind-spot. Hands-free if and only if a certified/validated supplier configuration can be licensed.
5. **Cost:** independent costed bill of materials and warranty/after-sales sensitivity at multiple assembly volumes. Avoid assuming every expensive feature can fit at $35k.
6. **Pedestrian compatibility:** front end engineered for realistic crash injury reduction, not boxier purely for intimidating appearance.
7. **Repairability:** repair cost and access quantified with insurers and independent repair professionals.

## First buyer problem to answer

A Santa Fe already begins at about the proposed HAVEN Core starting price and has a strong IIHS award. HAVEN must earn buyer trust with superior practical family safety, documented ease of ownership and post-sale service, rather than relying on design art alone.
